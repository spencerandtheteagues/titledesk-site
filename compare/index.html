Where it fits

Each tool does its job. None of them executes the assignment.

Land teams already run on four kinds of software. Together they still leave the actual work — the reading, the extraction, the organizing, the assembly — on a landman's desk. That is the gap TitleDesk was built to fill.

Where TitleDesk is different by design

  • Runs on your computer. Documents stay local by default; the expensive reading costs nothing; it works offline.
  • Deterministic where it matters. Ownership arithmetic is exact fractions in code, not a model's estimate. A model can only add to what the parser found.
  • Provenance on every figure. Runsheet rows link to the page; reports refuse unsupported numbers; the audit log cannot be rewritten.
  • Your format, not ours. The Customization Lab configures filing, columns and deliverables to your shop in plain English.
  • Bring your own AI, or none. No company-wide token bill; no vendor model in the middle of your client's documents.

What TitleDesk is not

It is not a data platform, a system of record, a mapping product or a legal opinion. It complements ArcGIS rather than replacing it, imports from RRC rather than re-publishing it, and reads from your Drive rather than becoming it.

On real assignments completed by working landmen, TitleDesk's automated run takes about twenty minutes for work that takes a landman three to five days; the landman then reviews and certifies.
