From assignment letter to a package for review

1. Define the assignment and the client format

Start with the client's assignment letter, subject tract, source deed, research scope and deliverable requirements. TitleDesk organizes the requirements and the work plan. Company profiles configure folder names, filing rules and report columns through the Customization Lab.

2. Research and read the source records

Work with your held documents and configured county-records sources. Current integrations include CountyRecords.com, TexasFile and CourthouseDirect; access depends on your account, county coverage, document dates and installed release. Keep the original instruments alongside the project.

Local OCR processes PDF and multi-page TIFF scans on your computer with no per-page cloud OCR charge. Automated title examination uses your selected AI provider after consent and sends selected page images to that provider. Research purchases, subscriptions and AI usage are separate costs.

3. Build a runsheet you can check

Organize instruments chronologically with parties, recording references, document types and source links. Open the cited page to review a proposed reading. Missing records and chain gaps belong in the work queue so they can be resolved or clearly carried into the exceptions. Explore the runsheet workflow.

4. Work the ownership and net mineral acres

TitleDesk computes interests using exact fractions rather than repeated decimal rounding. For example, three accepted interests of 1/3 total exactly 1. The mineral ownership workspace connects calculations with the tract and the supporting instruments; professional review still determines what each conveyance means.

5. Track curative and assemble the deliverable

Keep title issues, evidence and proposed next steps with the assignment. Prepare runsheets, ownership schedules, reports and the submission folder in the company's configured format. Package checks help identify missing source references and incomplete output before you hand the work to your reviewer. See the workflow phase by phase.

What to check before choosing landman software

  • Your county and date range. Confirm that the needed indexes and original instruments are available, including older references and separate record series.
  • Source evidence. Ask to open the actual page behind a runsheet row, an ownership interest and a curative exception.
  • Your client's workbook. Use a real blank template in the demonstration. Check column mapping, accepted corrections and the final folder structure.
  • Full operating cost. Include software seats, record-provider access, document charges, AI usage, setup and professional review. See current TitleDesk pricing and the ROI assumptions.
  • Completion and recovery. Include a missing instrument and an interrupted session in the evaluation. Ask the software to show what remains unresolved.
