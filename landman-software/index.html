Landman software

Landman software that does the reading, so you keep the judgment.

TitleDesk Agent is desktop landman software for oil and gas title work. It reads the county packet on your own computer, builds a source-linked runsheet, computes ownership in exact fractions, tracks curative, and assembles the folder your client actually accepts — in your company's format.

What landman software has to do — and what most of it skips

Land teams use a mix of records access, title analysis and administration tools. TitleDesk’s role is a desktop research and review workflow. Confirm its fit on your own evidence.

How TitleDesk handles a title assignment

1. Take the assignment as data

Paste the assignment letter the client sent. TitleDesk reads it as data — never as instructions — extracts the requirements (county, abstract or survey, source deed, lessors to verify, acreage, budget), tells you plainly what it can and cannot do, and writes a two-column plan: its work and yours, running at the same time. Anything moved to your column is marked manual and is never touched automatically.

2. Read the documents locally

Scanned county records are OCR'd on the laptop with Tesseract. There is no OCR API, no internet dependency and no per-page cloud charge. Multi-page instruments are processed page by page; a page OCR cannot read is marked needs your eyes rather than recorded as blank. Twenty-three instrument types are classified and filed by your naming conventions. More on local OCR.

3. Build the work product

  • Runsheet — recorded instruments in chronological order with grantor, grantee, volume and page, instrument number and interest, examined rows tied to their source; index-only rows labelled as unexamined. Chain gaps are flagged, not hidden. Runsheet details.
  • Ownership by tract — mineral and leasehold interests as exact BigInt fractions, so 1/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 is exactly 1 and an over-conveyance is reported as an exact excess. Net mineral acres, leasehold and held-by-production included.
  • Curative and issues — unleased interests, unreleased prior liens and leases, probate and heirship gaps, assignment-chain defects, each with the document and page it came from and a proposed next step.
  • Wells, leases and production — Texas RRC data imported and matched to wells and leases with canonical API identity.

4. Deliver in the client's format

Reports are written into your company's own Excel template, column names matched by meaning, never overwriting a row a person corrected. Ten report types in Word, Excel, PDF or CSV. The submission package is the folder tree you actually turn in, every document filed where the client expects it, originals copied and never moved. If a report would ship empty, the build says so.

5. Verify before anything leaves

TitleDesk can save a local review package containing proposed ownership and unresolved questions. That package is not proof that research is complete. External delivery has separate checks on the evidence, ownership replay and package hashes; a withheld upload stays local with the unresolved items stated. The landman still reviews the result.

Why landmen choose desktop software over a cloud portal

Title chaining, abstraction and ownership tools already exist. TitleDesk focuses on a desktop workflow with local OCR, spending controls and a company-format review package. See the sourced comparison; capabilities overlap and no head-to-head advantage has been established.

Who it is for

Independent landmen running their own assignments, land departments that need repeatable output across every landman, and title shops delivering to clients with fixed deliverable formats. Solo seats and enterprise workspaces are both offered — see pricing.

A reliable typical assignment time has not been established. County coverage, document volume, scan quality, AI availability and human review all affect elapsed time.
