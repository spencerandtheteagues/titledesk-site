Curative title defects

A curative list is not a summary of everything odd on the index. It is the set of specific defects, each tied to the instrument that creates it, with the action that resolves it.

The common defect classes

DefectWhat it looks likeTypical cure
Unreleased deed of trust or vendor's lien on the tractA lien instrument in the chain, or filed under the abstract by a chain party, with no release matched to it.Obtain or confirm a recordable release or satisfaction.
Unreleased prior oil and gas leaseA lease within the lien window, or older with no release of record.Confirm expiration or obtain a release.
Probate gapAn estate conveying with no probate or determination of heirship of record.Probate, heirship determination or affidavit of heirship.
Marital / community-property confirmationThe source deed's grantees do not cover every named lessor (a spouse not in title).Confirm ownership; obtain a conveyance or ratification.
Patent-era gapNo indexed conveyance into the earliest grantee in the chain.Further research; affidavit or curative deed as appropriate.
Over-conveyanceInterests conveyed out exceed what was owned — an exact excess.Correction deed or stipulation of interest.

What not to put on the list

Liens the index cannot tie to the subject tract — a chain party's deed of trust on a different parcel in the same survey, or a personal tax lien with no tract — belong in the lien schedule, not on the curative list. Thirty generic "obtain release" lines drown the six that matter.

Writing an item

Each item names the instrument by citation and date, states the defect in one sentence, and proposes the next step. "Obtain or confirm a recordable release or satisfaction of DT 130/577" is workable; "check liens" is not.
